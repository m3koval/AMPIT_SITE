Monday’s Bank Delays: Verify the Payment Before You Send It Again

What Chase, Zelle, and U.S. Bank said on November 16, 2026, and a practical checklist for business owners managing delayed payments without duplicate transfers.

Monday’s reports need careful labels

On Monday, November 16, customers reported trouble with banking and payments. For an owner handling invoices, payroll, or a supplier deadline, the immediate concern is practical: did the transaction fail, remain pending, or complete without a clear confirmation? Those states require different actions.

Chase’s public statement said JPMorganChase experienced a temporary issue involving a software bug in on-premise hardware systems. It said there was no evidence the issue was cyber-related, some customers were taking longer to serve, and customers retained access to accounts and funds. Chase also said transactions involving other banks could be affected where it was a party, while describing the problem as not systemic. Those are the bank’s statements, not an independent technical audit.

The statement said Chase was working to resolve the issue by the end of the day. That is a target, not confirmation of restoration. This article does not declare the incident over or promise a completion time for an individual payment.

Reported trouble is not the same as a bank-wide outage

ABC News’ live reporting, carried by ABC7, listed user reports involving Chase, Bank of America, U.S. Bank, Wells Fargo, and Zelle, among others. User-submitted outage reports are useful signals, but they cannot establish that every named institution’s own systems failed or that any institution was breached.

The same reporting quoted Wells Fargo’s website acknowledging technical issues. It also carried important distinctions: Zelle said its network was operating as it should, while issues at financial institutions could affect customers’ payments. U.S. Bank said its systems were not directly impacted, but clients could experience delays sending or receiving Zelle payments with another bank.

For Bank of America, the user reports in that coverage should remain labeled as reports, not converted into a confirmed bank-wide outage. Likewise, a delay involving another institution does not mean both banks suffered the same internal failure. Nothing in the sources reviewed establishes that Monday’s banking problem was caused by the earlier Microsoft or FAA incidents.

Before retrying, establish the transaction state

First, open your bank’s app or a saved, known website address. Review the transaction history and any official service notice. Capture the transaction reference, submission time, amount, intended recipient, and displayed status in your normal secure accounting records. Do not put account numbers or screenshots containing sensitive details into public posts.

If the result is unclear, contact the bank through an established channel, such as the number on your card or an authenticated support route. Ask whether the transaction is pending, completed, rejected, or safe to resubmit. Avoid repeatedly pressing Send simply because the confirmation screen is slow. Duplicate instructions can create a second problem after the first one clears.

For payroll and supplier payments, check the actual deadline and notify the responsible payroll provider, accountant, or vendor early. Explain what you know and when you will update them. Do not promise settlement solely because an app accepted a request. Keep the original approval record attached to any decision to use a different payment method.

Do not let urgency change who gets paid

An outage does not justify accepting new bank details from an unexpected message. If a vendor requests a different account or an employee asks for an urgent reroute, verify it through a previously established phone number and follow your normal approval process. Do not use the number supplied in the change request as your only check.

A backup banking relationship or alternate payment method can help some businesses, but it needs planning, authorization, and testing before an emergency. Different apps may still depend on the same institution or payment route. Discuss suitable options with your bank and financial advisers instead of assuming a second icon on your phone creates true independence.

Close the loop after access improves

Once service looks normal, reconcile the affected transactions against bank records and your accounting system. Check for duplicates, missing confirmations, late fees, and recipient follow-up. Save support references where a deadline or charge needs review. A functioning login is the beginning of reconciliation, not the end.

Owner takeaway

A delayed transaction is not proof of a breach. Confirm its state, preserve approvals, and keep customers informed.
