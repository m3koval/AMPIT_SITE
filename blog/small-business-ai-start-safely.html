How a Small Business Should Start Using AI (Without Leaking Client Data)

Your team is probably already using AI. The question is whether it is saving time on approved tools, or quietly moving client data into free accounts.

Start small, start safe, and measure the hours saved. Then grow.

1. Find out what is already in use

Ask your team which AI tools they use and what they use them for. Do not make it a punishment. The answers show you where AI is already saving time, and where client or company data is going into personal or free accounts.

2. Pick approved tools on business plans

Business plans of the major AI tools, like ChatGPT business plans, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Gemini for Google Workspace, come with data terms meant for companies. Free personal accounts are different. Check the data terms of any plan before client information goes into it.

3. Write a one-page AI policy

Keep it short: which tools are approved, what never goes into AI, who to ask, and how work gets reviewed before it goes to a client. Law firms should know that ABA Formal Opinion 512 calls for informed client consent before confidential information goes into self-learning AI tools (ABA Op. 512).

4. Start with one task that eats hours

Pick one: meeting notes, first drafts of routine emails, summarizing long documents or intake forms. Measure the time saved for a month before buying more licenses.

5. Clean up file sharing first

Tools like Microsoft 365 Copilot can surface anything a person already has access to. If files were shared too widely over the years, AI makes that easier to find. Tighten sharing before you roll it out.

6. Show people how

Most AI rollouts stall from lack of setup and training, not the tool. Give each person two or three real tasks to use it for, and turn on two-step login for every account that touches AI.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to use ChatGPT at work?
It depends on the plan and the data. Use a business plan with company data terms, and keep client confidential information out of personal or free accounts.
Should we turn on Microsoft 365 Copilot?
Clean up file sharing first. Copilot can surface anything a person already has access to.
Do we need an AI policy?
Yes. A one-page policy covering approved tools, what never goes in, and review rules is enough to start, and clients and insurers increasingly ask about it.
