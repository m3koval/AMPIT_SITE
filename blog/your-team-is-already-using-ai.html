AI Chats Can Create Privilege and Discovery Risks

By AMP IT · Charlotte, NC May 4, 2026 6 min read

A business plan is not a legal shield. In United States v. Heppner, the court ruled on February 10, 2026, with written reasons issued February 17, that the defendant’s particular Claude exchanges were not protected by attorney-client privilege or work product. The result turned on the facts and applicable legal rules. Counsel should assess the workflow before confidential legal material enters an AI service.

What the ruling establishes—and what it does not

The February 17 memorandum addressed exchanges the defendant initiated without counsel directing the work. The court considered the lack of an attorney-client relationship with Claude, confidentiality under the consumer policy at issue, and the purpose and direction of the work. It said the issue appeared to be one of first impression; it did not decide every attorney-supervised enterprise AI workflow.

Attorney-client privilege and work-product protection are distinct doctrines. A confidentiality agreement may be relevant to a workflow, but buying a subscription does not automatically create either protection. This article is not a comprehensive survey of later cases or a prediction of how a court will treat your records.

Find out where business information goes

Ask which AI accounts people use, what they submit, which files or apps are connected, and who can retrieve the results. Personal accounts and unmanaged connectors can move information outside approved processes. Do not assume that a familiar product name identifies the contract or configuration in use.

A paid plan is not a confidentiality or privilege test

Training preferences, contractual confidentiality, retention, access controls and lawful disclosure are separate questions. Turning off model training does not stop legal process. Risk depends on the data, purpose, authorization, contract, configuration and applicable duties—not simply whether a plan is free or paid.

Check the exact product terms and settings

Business terms can provide different training, confidentiality, retention and administrative commitments. They do not eliminate legal process, automatically preserve privilege, or make every data use permissible. Verify the exact product contract and settings with counsel and your data owner.

  • Anthropic commercial services: Commercial terms treat customer content as confidential and prohibit model training on it, but include a required-by-law or court/administrative-order disclosure exception. Check which terms govern the actual service.
  • Microsoft Copilot: Check the specific product’s contractual protections, processing boundaries and connected services; do not assume every feature keeps all processing inside a tenant.
  • Google Gemini: Access depends on the product, account and enabled connections. Google’s January 14, 2026 Personal Intelligence announcement describes app connections as off by default and opt-in. Previously enabled connections can persist. Review actual connected-app settings rather than assuming universal access or isolation; consumer Personal Intelligence and Workspace features are not the same product.
  • Other AI products: Verify the account’s applicable agreement, training and retention settings, subprocessors, sharing and connectors before approving confidential use. A product comparison is not legal clearance.

Match obligations to your role and information

Law firms must assess confidentiality and professional duties; regulated financial firms must assess their applicable rules, including FINRA requirements where applicable. HIPAA applies where the business is a covered entity or business associate, not to every CPA or adviser simply by occupation. See HHS guidance on covered entities and business associates. Ask counsel which obligations govern the specific workflow and whether client consent or other authorization is required.

Make approval testable

  1. Inventory accounts and data. Name a business data owner and legal reviewer. Identify permitted and prohibited data categories and the exact approved accounts.
  2. Review contracts and connections. Check retention, training terms, permissions, connectors and deletion commitments. Disable unneeded connections and confirm licensing for the features actually used.
  3. Document and teach the rules. Write an approved-tool policy, require human review of outputs, and give staff an escalation route for uncertain or accidental disclosures. Policy existence is not proof of enforcement or a predictable defense against negligence.
  4. Test before confidential use. Use approved samples to verify that unauthorized staff cannot retrieve sensitive content. Record the account, configuration, test result and reviewer; stop rollout and escalate failed tests. Recheck after permission or connector changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an enterprise AI plan preserve attorney-client privilege?

Not automatically. Contract protections can improve confidentiality but do not create privilege, work-product protection or immunity from legal process. Counsel must evaluate the facts and workflow.

Does opting out of training prevent disclosure?

No. Model training and legally required disclosure are different issues. Check retention and disclosure provisions as well as training settings.

Does Gemini connect to all my apps by default?

Do not assume universal access or isolation. Google describes Personal Intelligence app connections as opt-in and off by default; previously enabled connections can persist. Check the actual product, account and connected-app settings.

What is shadow AI?

AI use outside the organization’s approved accounts, tools or processes. Inventory actual use and data handling rather than assuming a written policy prevents it.
