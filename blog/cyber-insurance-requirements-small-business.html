Cyber Insurance Applications: Prepare Evidence, Not Assumptions

Cyber-insurance applications can ask for evidence of security controls. Requirements vary by insurer, policy and application. Obtain the actual questionnaire early and have your broker clarify ambiguous terms; answer from verified coverage, including exclusions and exceptions.

MFA Is The Baseline

Use the application’s exact definition of MFA coverage. Inventory email, VPN, remote access, admin portals and cloud systems, then document enforcement and exceptions. Ask your broker to clarify ambiguous wording rather than assuming what the insurer means.

Check Endpoint Detection Requirements

If the questionnaire asks about endpoint detection and response (EDR) or managed detection, verify the required product, covered devices and who handles alerts. Basic antivirus is not evidence that an EDR requirement is met.

Backups Need Proof

We have backups is not the same as recoverability. Carriers and owners both care about whether backups are isolated, tested, recent enough to matter, and watched by someone accountable.

Documentation Matters

Create an evidence table with the exact question, covered systems, control owner, dated evidence and unresolved exceptions. Have the business signatory and broker review it before submission. An assessment supports accurate answers; only the insurer determines coverage under the policy.

A control that exists but is undocumented can still create problems during renewal, a claim, or an audit. Write down what is enabled, who owns it, how alerts are handled, and when it was last reviewed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which controls should we document?
Use your insurer’s actual questionnaire. Map each requested control to covered systems, dated evidence, an owner and unresolved exceptions; do not substitute a generic checklist for the application.
Should we wait until renewal to check controls?
No. Review controls before renewal so there is time to fix gaps and document what is already in place.
